10 Money Mistakes Every Fresh Grad Makes
(So You Can Skip Them)

October 8, 2026·8 min read·by Bloom & Budget

Nobody teaches you this stuff, and then suddenly you're 23 with a credit score and no idea how it happened. The good news: every one of these mistakes has a cheap fix if you catch it early. Here are the ten that cost people the most in their first working years.

1. Treating your first salary like a windfall

First paycheck energy is real, and it usually ends in a cart full of things you didn't know you needed. Celebrate the moment — one nice thing, one nice dinner — then set up your budget the same week. The salary is recurring. The spending spree shouldn't be.

2. Not reading your payslip

Taxes, pension deductions, insurance premiums — your payslip is the most important document you ignore. Five minutes of reading it tells you where your money actually goes, and whether your employer deducted the right things. Weird deductions happen more than you'd think.

3. Lifestyle inflation, year one

You get paid more, so you upgrade everything: apartment, phone plan, gym, skincare. And somehow you feel poorer. The rule that fixes this: when your income goes up, let your spending go up slower. Bank the difference before it evaporates.

4. The subscription creep

Four streaming services, three “free trials” you forgot, an app you used once in March. Individually they're $5–15; together they're a phone bill. Put one calendar reminder every three months: cancel one subscription. You will always find one.

5. No emergency fund, because “nothing's happened yet”

The whole point of an emergency fund is that emergencies don't schedule a warning call. Even a small buffer — one month of rent — turns a crisis into an inconvenience. Start with $25 a paycheck if that's what you have. It compounds (see our full emergency fund guide).

6. Credit cards as free money

A credit card is a tool for building a credit history, not an extension of your salary. The one rule that keeps them safe: never swipe what you can't pay off in full when the statement comes. Rewards points mean nothing at 20%+ interest.

7. Waiting to invest until you “make real money”

Time is the only thing a bigger salary can't buy back. $50 a month from 23 beats $200 a month from 33 — the math on that is genuinely upsetting. You don't need to be an expert; you need to start small and boring and not touch it.

8. Ignoring taxes until the deadline

Then paying late fees, or missing deductions you were entitled to. Twenty minutes of setup in January saves a panic in April. If your situation is simple, a basic tax app does the job; if you have a side income, spend the money on an accountant once and learn from it.

9. Financing depreciating things

Phones, laptops, a car that's newer than your career — if it loses value and you're paying interest on it, it's double damage. Buy last year's model in cash where you can. Nobody at the party asks what year your phone is.

10. Comparing your chapter 1 to someone's chapter 9

Social media shows you holidays, handbags, and apartments from people who might be funding all three with debt. The most expensive mistake in your twenties is spending money to look like people whose finances you can't see. Run your own race with your own spreadsheet.

You're not behind. You're just early. Everyone you're comparing yourself to started at exactly the same place.

The fix for all ten

Most of these come down to two habits: know your numbers, and automate the boring parts. Awareness plus automation beats willpower every single time.
