Your Emergency Fund on an Entry-Level Salary: The Realistic Guide

“Save 3–6 months of expenses” is cute advice when rent eats half your paycheck. Here is the version that actually works on a starter salary.

October 3, 20267 min read

← Back to all posts

How to build a cushion on a small salary

"Three to six months of expenses" is the standard advice and it is useless in month one, when the real choice is between $200 and nothing. Start at $500, then one month of essentials, then three. Each of those is a milestone you can actually picture.

The mechanics matter more than the amount: a separate account, a transfer the day after payday, and one rule that the money is only for things that are genuinely unexpected and genuinely urgent. A sale is neither, and neither is a wedding you knew about for six months.

When rent eats half your paycheck

You are not failing at saving, you are saving at the hardest possible starting point. Aim for the smallest amount you will not cancel in March, and let it run while you work on the bigger levers: rent, the commute, and the income itself.
